    Kalyoncu (2025) formulas¶

    Kalyoncu (2025) proposes four readability formulas for Turkish (K1–K4). Their output is "text difficulty": a higher value means a harder text. The tool gives the four formulas and their five components in the readability group. These keys are produced for Turkish only.

    Key Kalyoncu's name Definition Source
    kalyoncu_kl KL words not in the list / words × 100 p.71–72
    kalyoncu_ttr TTR stems occurring once / words × 100 p.73
    kalyoncu_kco KCO complex sentences / sentences × 100 p.73
    kalyoncu_yco YCO verbals / sentences × 10 p.74
    kalyoncu_ocu OCU words / sentences p.77
    kalyoncu_k1 … kalyoncu_k4 First … Fourth formula below p.97–99

    kalyoncu_ttr is not the tool's ttr: it measures the share of stems that occur only once, not the share of distinct words. Kalyoncu calls this measure TTR.

    Formulas¶

    Key Formula (Tables 31–34)
    kalyoncu_k1 −29.829 + 0.508·KL + 1.191·TTR + 0.733·KCO − 0.66·OCU
    kalyoncu_k2 25.457 + 0.877·KL + 0.668·TTR + 0.333·OCU
    kalyoncu_k3 25.153 + 0.834·KL + 0.686·TTR + 0.226·YCO
    kalyoncu_k4 43.368 + 1.008·KL + 0.624·OCU

    If a component is nan, the formula is nan.

    Word and sentence¶

    The word is the tool's default word, with one difference: a word with digits is read aloud and counts as the words of its reading (Kalyoncu p.xiii). 1968 is "bin dokuz yüz altmış sekiz", 5 words; %50 is "yüzde elli", 2 words. Abbreviations are not expanded. This rule applies to the Kalyoncu keys only.

    So kalyoncu_ocu can differ from the tool's sent_len_mean:

    Text sent_len_mean kalyoncu_ocu
    Bu bir tümce. 1999. Bitti. 5 / 2 = 2.5 9 / 2 = 4.5

    The sentence is the tool's default sentence (Concepts); a sentence without a letter is not counted. Kalyoncu splits sentences by meaning: sentences with their own meaning inside quotation marks or as an aside, and statements and explanations after a colon, are separate sentences (p.xii). This rule is not applied; meaning cannot be checked automatically. On TOMA's 57 texts the tool's sentence rule differs from the expert's count by 41 sentences in total (about 2%) and matches exactly in 37 texts.

    The stem is Zeyrek's lemma plus derivational suffixes: kitapçılar → kitapçı, öğretmenliği → öğretmenlik. Nothing is added to the stem after the first inflectional suffix: oradaki → ora, karşısındaki → karşı. Causative and passive suffixes, verbal suffixes, compound-verb suffixes (-ebil, -iver, -eyaz, -akal, -akoy, -adur, -egel) and the copula are not part of the stem either: küçültülmek → küçül, gelebildi → gel, evdeydi → ev. If Zeyrek gives several analyses, the choice follows "Choosing the analysis".

    KL: matching against the list¶

    The list is Kalyoncu's familiarity-based word list (Appendix 1, pp.129–160, 4,600 words). A word is in the list if one of three forms is: the word itself lowercased, its stem, or, for a verb, stem + -mak/-mek. A softened final consonant (öğretmenliğ) is also tried hardened. Circumflexes are removed on both sides: hikaye and hikâye in a text both match the list entry. KL looks at familiarity; where the form without a circumflex is another word (hala/hâlâ, dahi/dâhi), both forms are known words, so the result does not change. Circumflexes are removed in KL only, nowhere else, kalyoncu_ttr included.

    Kalyoncu's nine rules (p.71–72):

    Rule In the tool
    1. Proper nouns do not count as outside the list applied: a word tagged PROPN, or capitalised and not at the start of a sentence; stays in the denominator, not in the numerator
    2. An inflected form counts as the list word applied: the stem
    3. Common derivations do not count as outside the list not applied: which suffixes count as common is not stated
    4. List verbs used as verbals applied: Zeyrek's verbal tags
    5. Onomatopoeic words do not count as outside the list not applied
    6. Intensified forms (büsbüyük) not applied
    7. Causative and passive forms applied: Zeyrek's voice tags
    8. A list word used in a rare sense counts as outside the list not applied: needs reading the context
    9. Numbers do not count as outside the list applied: numbers written in digits and in words count as in the list

    Proper nouns¶

    A proper noun is a word spaCy tags PROPN, or a word that starts with a capital letter and is not at the start of a sentence. Forms with an apostrophe are included (İstanbul'da, İstanbul’da). A name at the start of a sentence is a proper noun only if tagged PROPN. Reason: the spaCy Turkish model does not tag Derya, İstanbul'da, Almanya'da or Hassan'ı as PROPN, and Turkish spelling capitalises proper nouns. This rule applies in the kalyoncu_* keys only.

    Numbers written in words¶

    Kalyoncu's rule 9 does not tell digits from words (p.72). iki, otuz and dokuz are in the list too, and so are their inflected (ikisi), ordinal (üçüncü) and distributive (beşer) forms. A word is a number if:

    • spaCy tagged it NUM;
    • the root of the selected Zeyrek analysis is a number (Num);
    • spaCy tagged it noun, adjective, adverb or X, and the root of one of its Zeyrek analyses is a number (spaCy can tag beşer as a noun).

    A pronoun, verb or proper-noun tag decides: onun and ona are not "on". A number word counts as a word in the word count and in TTR like any other word.

    Choosing the analysis¶

    Zeyrek can give several analyses for a word. In the kalyoncu_* keys the first analysis whose type matches spaCy's word class is taken: PRON pronoun, NOUN and PROPN noun, VERB and AUX verb, ADJ adjective, ADV adverb, NUM number. If none matches, the first analysis is taken. The stem, the verbal and the complex-sentence counts (TTR, KCO, YCO) come from the selected analysis. Example: for onun spaCy says PRON, so the pronoun analysis is taken, not Zeyrek's first one, the verb "onmak". The rest of the tool keeps the "first analysis" rule.

    Verbals and complex sentences¶

    A verbal is a word whose Zeyrek analysis has an infinitive (-mak, -ma, -ış), participle (-an, -dık, -acak, -mış, -ar/-maz) or converb (-ip, -arak, -ınca, -ken, -madan, -dıkça…) tag. The -ken of a noun with the copula (öğrenciyken) does not count. A word counts as one verbal at most.

    A complex sentence (KCO) has at least one verbal or at least two verbs. Kalyoncu defines it as a sentence "with more than one judgement, containing a verbal, or joined to another" (p.73). He does not define "judgement", but in his CYargı variable he measures it by counting verbs and verbals (p.73); the tool counts it the same way. A verb is a word whose last type in Zeyrek's analysis is Verb (evdeydi included). Most sentences joined by a conjunction contain two verbs and are caught; nominal sentences without a copula (Hava güzel ama soğuk.) are not.

    Grade levels¶

    The tool gives the number only. Kalyoncu's grade-level tables (p.102–105):

    Level K1 K2 K3 K4
    Before grade 3 <30 <35 <35 <40
    Grades 3–4 30–40 35–45 35–45 40–50
    Grades 5–6 40–50 45–55 45–55 50–55
    Grades 7–8 50–60 55–60 55–60 55–59
    Grades 9–10 60–70 60–64 60–65 59–65
    Grades 11–12 70–76 64–70 65–70 65–72
    Undergraduate 76–85 70–85 70–85 72–85
    Postgraduate >85 >85 >85 >85

    The text and the tables of the source differ in four places; the values come from the tables:

    • K1: the text says "70–75 grades 11–12, 76–85 undergraduate", the table says "70–76".
    • K4: the text says "50–59" for grades 7–8, the table says "55–59".
    • Which range a boundary value belongs to is not stated; the text says "30 and below", the table "<30".
    • The text refers to the tables as 37–40; their captions say 36–39.

    Differences from TOMA¶

    TOMA (Kalyoncu et al. 2026) is a desktop program that computes K1–K4 automatically.

    • TOMA also removes circumflexes in KL (p.298); here the two agree.
    • TOMA leaves words made only of digits out of KL (p.298). The tool reads them aloud and counts them as in the list; it counts numbers written in words as in the list too.
    • TOMA computes KCO and YCO with Stanza dependency parsing; the tool uses Zeyrek.
    • TOMA does not say which tool splits sentences for the main counts.

    Limitations¶

    These values approximate Kalyoncu's hand counts.

    • KL rests on expert judgement: rules 3, 5 and 8 are not applied. KL is therefore not expected to match the expert values exactly; the tool counts more words as outside the list than the expert does.
    • Proper nouns follow the capital letter: a name at the start of a sentence that is not tagged PROPN can count as outside the list; any other capitalised word in mid-sentence (a title, an emphasis) counts as a proper noun.
    • Suffixes Zeyrek does not know (-alı, -ası, -casına, -maksızın) cannot be counted as verbals.
    • If the selected Zeyrek analysis is wrong, so is the result (the first one when none matches spaCy's class): okuma is analysed as "ok-um-a", not as an infinitive; the participle in okunur kitap counts as a finite verb.
    • For a word Zeyrek cannot analyse, the stem is the word itself lowercased; foreign words and misspellings count as a separate stem in each form.
    • KCO is the least exact component. In TOMA too, KCO is the variable furthest from the expert values (Kalyoncu et al. 2026).

    The list¶

    The list is in turkish_linguistic_features/data/kalyoncu_2025_ek1.txt, in Kalyoncu's order and spelling (circumflexes included). It is not covered by the package's MIT licence; its rights belong to its author. The PDF's text layer corrupts ğ, ş, ı and initial İ/Ş, so every entry was checked against the page image. The source spells "Çita" with a dotted i, and it was left so. The file was compared again, entry by entry and in order, with Appendix 1 of Kalyoncu's thesis: all 4,600 entries are the same; the only difference is that the source's "Kılmak(Namaz)" is written "kılmak" (noted at the top of the file).
